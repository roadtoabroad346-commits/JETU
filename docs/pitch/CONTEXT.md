## 1. Positioning

**Jetu Admissions** is presented as a platform with AI tools that brings high-school
students in Central Asia through overseas admission and scholarship applications.
The slide is dated **July 2026**. `jetu.kz` appears in mock browser headers for

## 2. Problem

The pitch describes applying abroad alone as difficult because information is
fragmented, scams exist and students lack experience. It claims consulting can
cost up to **2,000,000 ₸**, and cites **170,000+ graduates**, with insufficient
consultant capacity, especially in the regions. The slide does not identify the
geography/source/year of that graduate statistic. These figures require sourcing
before use as verified public marketing claims.

## 5. Market model

- **TAM:** $10+ billion, described as the worldwide overseas-admissions preparation market.
- **SAM:** approximately 800,000 school graduates per year in Central Asia.
- **SOM:** a five-year goal of 500,000 users × 6% conversion × $40 ARPU ≈ $1.2 million
  annual revenue; described as less than 1% of the Central Asian market.

The arithmetic yields $1.2 million, but the slide provides no underlying sources
or demonstrated conversion, ARPU or user acquisition. TAM is monetary, SAM is a
population and SOM is projected revenue, so they are not directly comparable
market-share measures. Do not infer present revenue, subscribers or traction.

## 6. Business model

Freemium with three tiers and monthly subscriptions positioned as cheaper than
one consultant hour:

| Tier   | Pitch price | Proposed entitlement                                        |
| ------ | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Free   | Free        | Guides and minimal tools                                    |
| Plus   | 2,990 ₸     | Unlimited AI: matching, essays, deadlines and college list  |
| Mentor | 14,990 ₸    | Everything plus application review/support by a live mentor |

B2B offerings are proposed for schools, colleges and educational centers. These
are July 2026 pricing/entitlement hypotheses, not authorization to implement billing,
publish an unlimited-usage promise or grant paid capabilities without cost analysis.
